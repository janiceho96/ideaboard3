# Qualitative review of the first 12 live drafts

This is a Codex subagent review of actual provider output against the corpus rubrics. It is **not a human score, a broad model accuracy estimate, or evidence that a draft was applied successfully to a real board**.

The review is bounded to the first 12 completed records in `output/assistant-generation/live-results.jsonl`, from `D001` at `2026-10-16T08:50:43Z` through `B004` at `2026-10-16T08:51:43Z`. Later bulk-run records are excluded. All 12 identify the model as `gpt-6-luna`. Route and frontend-validation evidence comes from the corresponding pilot records in `output/assistant-generation/evaluated-results.json`; prompt requirements come from the three corpus JSON files beside this document.

**Complete** means the requested content and constraints are satisfied. **Partial** means useful requested material is present, but a material requirement is missing. **Failed** means a central instruction is contradicted or the requested artifact is replaced by a materially different result. Delivery is reported separately: useful text in a rejected payload is not a working feature.

## What this pilot establishes

- The original essay request and its paraphrase produce useful ideas, but neither supplies the exact three-section reusable essay design. One substitutes First Principles; the other substitutes four-section OODA. Both lose the distinction between framework guidance, empty answer areas, and a separate chat answer.
- When the provider follows an exact custom shape more closely, such as B001 or D004, the native/frontend contract can still reject it. This is different from failing to understand the subject.
- Advice can be semantically useful while the chosen action is wrong. C003 answers the question inside a proposed note; C004 chooses and rewrites a framework instead of resolving the ambiguous target.
- No reviewed response asserts invented personal project outcomes or metrics as facts. Placeholders and missing-evidence language are helpful, but do not compensate for action, structure, or authorization failures.

## Limits

This review covers a small, deliberately early pilot dominated by new-framework requests. It does not establish performance on all generated requests, locked notes, long sources, other models, or ordinary non-AI canvas operations. Existing note locks are not exercised by these 12 cases. The review reads saved payloads and validation results; it does not inspect rendered layouts, edit real boards, verify recovery/Undo, or make additional provider calls. A native-valid result means native parsing/validation accepted it, and frontend-valid means the local frontend validator accepted it. Neither is proof that the requested behavior was complete or that board application succeeded.
